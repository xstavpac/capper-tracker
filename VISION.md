# Capper Tracker — Founder Story & Vision

## Why this exists

I built this for myself first. I wanted a place to track the sports
betting cappers I follow — across Twitter, Discord, Telegram, DubClub,
wherever — because I had no real way to know who actually made me money
and who was just posting highlights.

What I didn't expect: I got hooked on it. I check my numbers every day
now. It's become part of my routine — not because I have to, but because
I want to know. That's the whole signal. If I stopped using this and the
data disappeared, I'd genuinely be upset about it. That reaction is the
product.

## The core insight

Cappers can hide losses, delete bad picks, and only showcase their
winners. Bettors following them have no independent, permanent record of
how a capper actually performs over time — they only have what the
capper chooses to show them.

This app flips that. The bettor keeps their own record, on their own
terms, that nobody else can edit or delete.

## The psychology behind why people will pay

Give someone a large free allowance up front — 1,000 tracked picks — so
they can build out their own history for the cappers they already
follow. The moment they've invested real time logging that data, they
have something to protect. Walking away means losing it. Once someone's
hooked the way I got hooked, $5/month to keep their own history isn't a
real decision — it's nothing compared to what they already spend
following picks in the first place.

This isn't a hypothesis. I am the first data point. I built it, I use it
daily, and I would pay to keep my data if I had to. The bet is that
enough other bettors who follow cappers will feel the same pull once
they've put in a few weeks of tracking.

## The business model

**Free tier**: 1,000 tracked picks. Enough to build a real, meaningful
history across multiple cappers before hitting a wall.

**Pro tier**: $4.99/month. Low enough that it doesn't require a real
purchasing decision from someone who already spends money on picks.

**Future tier**: $9.99–14.99/month — advanced analytics, custom
dashboards, alerts, deeper capper breakdowns, professional tools.

Rough revenue math at scale:
- 1,000 subscribers × $5 = $5,000/month
- 10,000 subscribers × $5 = $50,000/month
- 50,000 subscribers × $5 = $250,000/month

Infrastructure stays lean at these scales (roughly $100–500/month in the
1,000–5,000 user range, $500–3,000+/month at 10,000–50,000 users on
current hosting), so the margin holds up even before optimizing costs.

## The long-term vision: community verification

The bigger version of this product isn't just personal tracking — it's
a community-powered, transparent record of capper performance that
individual cappers can't manipulate.

If many different users independently log the same pick from the same
capper, the system recognizes it as verified and it becomes part of a
permanent, trustworthy record. Conflicting submissions get flagged,
compared by timestamp, and resolved. Picks get locked once game results
are known, so nothing can be edited after the fact to look better.

Cappers would be ranked not by follower count, but by a trust score
built from:
- Community verification rate
- Number of verified picks
- Long-term and recent performance
- Transparency
- Number of active users actually tracking them

Badges like "Community Verified," "1,000+ Verified Picks," "Trending
This Week," and "Positive ROI History" make trustworthy performance
visible at a glance.

The platform doesn't need to build this database itself — the users
build it, by using the product for their own reasons (tracking who makes
them money). The platform's job is just to organize, verify, and surface
what the community has already created.

## The end goal

Not a pick-selling service. Not a prediction service. The trusted place
bettors go to answer one question: **who actually performs over time,
and who is just showing highlights?**

## Where this stands today (as of this document)

This is the long-term direction, not the current build. The app today is
a private, single-user tracking tool — every user's data is scoped to
just them, with no shared/community layer yet. That's a deliberate,
solid foundation to build the community verification layer on top of
later, once the core personal-tracking product is proven out further.

The biggest open question before building the community layer isn't
pricing — it's getting people to actually log enough picks, easily
enough, that a real community dataset forms. The tracking experience
has to be fast enough that it doesn't feel like a chore, or the whole
verification model never gets enough data to work.
